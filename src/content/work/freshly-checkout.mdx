## Overview

Freshly is a direct-to-consumer subscription that delivers prepared meals. In August 2022, an Accenture assessment showed the site's conversion rate had fallen 22% year over year. I led the design of a homepage and checkout redesign to recover that performance, and along the way make it clearer what Freshly actually is.

## The problem

The numbers had a story behind them. Brand-awareness research found that 34% of consumers thought Freshly was a meal-kit service, which is a different product with different expectations. On top of that confusion, people found the homepage overwhelming. Packaging, pricing, and subscription details were all unclear, and the page repeated itself while leaving out the things visitors actually needed to decide.

> A third of visitors didn't know what they were looking at. No funnel survives that.

## Research goals

Before redesigning anything, we set three research objectives grounded in the data:

- Understand what people want to know about Freshly before they commit to a subscription.
- Find the friction points across the homepage and funnel that were costing us.
- Judge how well the current design actually drove conversion, and where the openings were.

## Approach

We focused on where the money was leaking. Mobile had the steepest drop-off compared to web, so the mobile homepage and checkout became the priority. The goal was to cut drop-off by more than 3% across the stages, and by 2% in the most efficient paths, while tackling a painful 50% drop at the meal-selection step.

## The redesign

After several rounds of iteration, the design that landed did a few specific things:

- Cut the page height down so people engaged instead of scrolling past.
- Said plainly that Freshly is a subscription, which cleared up the meal-kit confusion.
- Added a live pricing calculator so the cost model was transparent instead of guessed at.
- Used consistent calls to action so there was always one obvious next step.

## Testing

We tested the new homepage with users before shipping. They came away understanding Freshly's value proposition, and the live pricing and easy-to-find answers to common questions drew specific praise. That gave us the confidence to run it as a live conversion test.

## Results

The A/B test compared the new experience against the baseline, following new users from homepage to completed order. It reached statistical significance quickly: a 24.1% improvement in conversion, with a 99.6% probability of beating the baseline. Drop-off into the "choose meals" flow fell noticeably at the same time.

## Reflection

The biggest lesson was that a conversion problem often isn't a conversion problem. People weren't abandoning because the funnel had too many steps. They were abandoning because they didn't understand the product or trust the price. Fixing clarity did more for the numbers than any single funnel tweak could have.
